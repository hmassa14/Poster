# Style guide

This is the house voice of *This Week in AI*. The writer, critic, and SEO
stages all read this file. The feedback stage appends to the
"Learned rules" section at the bottom; do not hand-edit that section.

## Voice

- **Register:** a sharp, well-read analyst briefing a colleague. Direct,
  specific, unhurried. Dry humor is allowed in small doses; jokes never replace
  a fact.
- **Person:** third person for reporting. "We" is allowed for editorial
  judgement ("We think the interesting part is the pricing, not the model").
  Never "I". Never claim to have used, tested, or felt anything.
- **Tense:** past tense for events, present tense for what things are and mean.
- **Density:** every paragraph carries at least one specific fact (a number, a
  name, a date, a quoted phrase). Cut any sentence that could be deleted without
  losing information.
- **Uncertainty:** say what is known, what is claimed, and what is unknown, in
  that order. "OpenAI says" and "according to the model card" are load-bearing
  phrases, not hedges.

## Sentences and paragraphs

- Lead with the fact, then the context, then the implication.
- Paragraphs are 2 to 5 sentences. One idea per paragraph.
- Numbers are specific and sourced. "About 40%" only when the source says
  "about". Never round for effect. Keep benchmark names and conditions.
- Links go on the noun they support ("the [model card](url) lists..."), never
  on "here" or "this".
- Bold is for the headline of an item in a list, never for emphasis mid-sentence.
- Em dashes are fine in moderation. Semicolons are fine. Exclamation marks are not.
- Headings are plain nouns or questions. Never "5 things", never clickbait.

## Words we do not use

The deterministic linter enforces `publication/banned_phrases.txt`. The spirit:
no AI-marketing vocabulary ("game-changer", "revolutionary", "seamless",
"unlock", "leverage" as a verb, "deep dive", "in today's fast-paced world"),
no throat-clearing ("It's worth noting", "Interestingly", "Notably"), no
false-balance closers ("only time will tell", "the future remains to be seen").

## Sourcing rules

- Primary source first: the vendor's own post, the paper, the filing, the docs.
- Secondary reporting (Reuters, The Information, Bloomberg, The Verge) is cited
  when it is the only source, and named as such: "Reuters reported...".
- A claim with no source in the research pack does not go in the issue.
- Quotes are exact and attributed by name and role.
- When two sources disagree, say so and link both.

## Structure rules

- Follow `publication/format.md` exactly. Section headings match it.
- The lead story explains *why it matters* in concrete terms: what changes for
  a practitioner, a buyer, or a builder.
- Every item in "Also this week" has a link in its first sentence.
- End on the last item, not on a summary or a sign-off.
