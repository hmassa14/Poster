# The Price Cut That Reads Like a Confession

*This week the cost of running models fell faster than the models improved, and the vendors started showing their work.*

## The week in one paragraph

The most consequential number this week was not a benchmark score but a price. Halcyon Labs cut Tern 3 input pricing by 60%, from $2.50 to $1.00 per million tokens, and published a [latency page](https://halcyon.example/tern-3/latency) alongside it, which is the part that changes how a buyer should read the cut. Around it, the pattern held: Orvex put Marlin-27B under Apache 2.0 after a licence that had kept its predecessor out of commercial deployments, Cinder Compute opened 40,000 accelerators in Alberta at prices it says undercut the largest clouds, and Vantage Systems made agent audit logs standard for every enterprise tenant. The one regulator in the mix, the Nordic Digital Authority, issued its first fine under its automated-decision rules. Cost, capacity, and accountability all moved in the same direction, and the vendors, for once, published the numbers a practitioner would need to check them.

## The lead: Halcyon cuts Tern 3 input pricing by 60% and shows its latency

Halcyon Labs announced on September 8 that Tern 3 input tokens now cost $1.00 per million, down from $2.50, with output pricing unchanged at $10.00 per million, according to the company's [pricing update](https://halcyon.example/blog/tern-3-pricing). It is the first price change since Tern 3 launched in May 2026, per the [model card](https://halcyon.example/tern-3/model-card). The cut took effect immediately.

The price is the headline, but the second half of the announcement is the part worth reading twice. Halcyon published a public [latency page](https://halcyon.example/tern-3/latency) that reports p50 and p99 time-to-first-token by context tier. At the 32K tier, p50 is 410 ms and p99 is 1.9 s. Halcyon had never published latency figures before; buyers relied on their own measurements or on secondhand reports. A vendor volunteering its p99 is rare, and it turns a pricing decision into something a team can model rather than guess at.

The company's framing was unusually plain. "We are pricing input tokens at what they cost us to serve plus a margin we can defend, and publishing the latency numbers so nobody has to take our word for it," said Priya Raman, Halcyon's head of platform, in the [pricing post](https://halcyon.example/blog/tern-3-pricing).

What changes for practitioners is the buy-versus-build calculation on retrieval-heavy workloads, where input tokens dominate spend. Halcyon claims that at the new price Tern 3 is cheaper than self-hosted Marlin-27B for retrieval workloads under 40M tokens per day. That is Halcyon's number, produced by Halcyon's own cost calculator, and it assumes a team without its own accelerators; the [pricing post](https://halcyon.example/blog/tern-3-pricing) does not show the calculation. But even discounted for the source, the direction is clear: the cost advantage of running an open-weight model yourself narrows sharply for teams whose volume sits below that line.

Read the two announcements together and the logic is visible. A price cut on its own invites the suspicion that quality or capacity was cut with it; publishing p50 and p99 by tier is the vendor's way of saying it was not, and it gives teams a number to hold Halcyon to. Output pricing staying at $10.00 per million also tells you where the margin now sits: the cut is aimed at workloads that read far more than they write, which is to say retrieval, summarisation, and anything that stuffs a context window, not chat.

Two things remain unknown. Halcyon did not say whether the cut applies to batch pricing, and the latency page has no figures for the 128K tier, which is exactly where long-context retrieval lives. A team deciding on the strength of this announcement is deciding on the 32K numbers and hoping the 128K ones hold. [Wiretap](https://wiretap.example/2026/09/08/halcyon-tern-3-price-cut) read the cut as a response to open-weight rivals closing the gap, which is plausible but is Wiretap's inference, not Halcyon's statement. What to watch: whether the 128K latency numbers appear, and whether competitors match the price or the transparency.

## Also this week

**Orvex releases Marlin-27B under Apache 2.0.** Orvex published the weights for Marlin-27B on September 4 under the Apache 2.0 licence, with a 256K token context window and a [technical report](https://orvex.example/marlin-27b/technical-report), according to the [release post](https://orvex.example/blog/marlin-27b). The licence is the story. The previous model, Marlin-12B, shipped under a [custom licence](https://orvex.example/marlin-12b/license) that barred commercial use above 10 million monthly active users, which kept it out of most production deployments. Marlin-27B has no such clause. The report does not disclose training data beyond "web, code, and licensed text", so teams with provenance requirements still have a question to ask.

**Nordic Digital Authority fines Brightline Recruit €4.2 million.** The Authority's [decision](https://nda.example/decisions/2026-091), issued September 5, found that Brightline Recruit screened 38,000 job applications with an AI model between January and June 2026 without telling applicants, in breach of its automated-decision rules. It is the first fine under those rules, which took effect in 2025, and the decision runs to the specific count of applications rather than a vague finding of non-compliance. "Automated rejection without disclosure is exactly the harm the rules were written to prevent," said Ingrid Solberg, the Authority's director of enforcement. Brightline says it [will appeal](https://brightline.example/press/response-nda); no timeline was given. Any company filtering people at scale in the Authority's jurisdiction now has a precedent with a number on it.

**Cinder Compute opens a 40,000-accelerator cluster in Alberta.** Cinder [announced](https://cinder.example/news/alberta-live) on September 9 that its Alberta site is live with 40,000 accelerators, priced at $1.85 per accelerator-hour on one-year commitments and $2.60 on demand. Its previous largest site had 12,000. Cinder says the on-demand price undercuts the three largest cloud providers' list prices for comparable hardware; that comparison is Cinder's own table, and the company did not say how much of the capacity is already reserved, which is the number that determines whether the price is available to anyone reading this.

**Vantage Systems ships agent audit logs to all enterprise tenants.** Per the [changelog](https://vantage.example/changelog/2026-09-07), every enterprise tenant now gets a record of each tool call an agent makes, with inputs, outputs, the policy that allowed it, and 90-day retention. The logs were previously a paid add-on with 30-day retention. Compliance teams that blocked agent deployments for lack of an audit trail lose that objection; export formats beyond CSV were not announced.

**Fennel Health explains how it evaluated a triage model before deployment.** In a conference talk on September 6, with [slides published](https://fennelhealth.example/talks/triage-eval-2026), Fennel Health's head of clinical AI described a 14-month evaluation that included a 2,000-case blinded comparison against nurses. The underlying case data is not public, but the protocol is, and it is a rare worked example of a pre-deployment evaluation in a regulated setting.

## Research corner

A [preprint](https://arxiv.example/abs/2609.01234) from Aldermoor University, posted September 3, tested four models of up to 70B parameters on the HaystackQA-2 benchmark with retrieved documents presented in six different orderings. Accuracy varied by up to 18 points depending on order, a larger swing than the gap between the smallest and largest model in the study. Position effects on single models were already known; the new result is the comparison of effect size against scale. The practical reading, which the authors state, is that the ordering step in a retrieval pipeline deserves more engineering attention than model selection does. The study stops at 70B; frontier hosted models were not tested, so whether the effect shrinks at larger scale is an open question. For anyone running retrieval today the immediate implication is cheap to act on: if the ordering step in your pipeline is whatever the vector store returned, that is a variable you have not measured, and the preprint suggests it may be the largest one you control.

## Signal vs. noise

The claim circulating this week is that Marlin-27B "beats Tern 3" on coding. Orvex's own [technical report](https://orvex.example/marlin-27b/technical-report) puts Marlin-27B at 71.2 on LedgerBench-Code, 1.4 points ahead of Tern 3, but that figure is pass@1 with 8 samples, while the Tern 3 [model card](https://halcyon.example/tern-3/model-card) reports pass@1 with 1 sample. The two numbers were produced under different conditions and do not support a head-to-head ranking. What the evidence supports is narrower: Marlin-27B is competitive on that benchmark under a sampling regime that favours it, and a like-for-like comparison has not been published by either company.
