# Editorial policy

## Sourcing

1. Every factual claim in an issue is attributable to a source the research
   stage fetched and recorded in the research pack. The writer has no web
   access, so it cannot add sources; the critic checks every number, date,
   name, price, benchmark, and quote against the pack; the link verifier
   flags any URL not in the pack. A claim without a source is removed, not
   hedged.
2. Primary sources come first: the vendor's own post, the paper, the filing,
   the docs, the regulator's decision. Secondary reporting is cited when it
   is the only source and is named as such ("Reuters reported").
3. Vendor claims are labeled as claims. Benchmark numbers carry the benchmark
   name and the conditions the source states. Numbers are never rounded for
   effect.
4. When sources disagree, the issue says so and links both.
5. Rumours, anonymous single-source reports, and leaks are not published as
   fact. They may appear in "Signal vs. noise" as claims, with what the
   evidence supports. A lead story cannot rest on an unverified central fact
   (escalation category `unverified_major_claim`).

## Attribution and quotes

6. Quotes are verbatim, attributed by name and role, and limited per the
   copyright policy (40 words each, two per source). Paraphrase is preferred
   for anything longer.
7. The issue never implies the system used, tested, or experienced anything.
   "We tested" is a forbidden assertion. The system reports what sources say.

## Judgement

8. Stories are ranked by consequence for practitioners, buyers, and builders,
   not by volume of coverage. Funding, hiring, and marketing without new
   capability are cut. The editor's brief records what was cut and why.
9. Stories covered in earlier issues are not re-run unless something
   material changed; the coverage index enforces this and the issue says what
   the callback is.
10. The publication takes positions about what matters and states
    uncertainty plainly: what is known, what is claimed, what is unknown.

## Disclosure

11. Every issue, page, and email carries the AI-authorship disclosure from
    `poster.yaml`. The renderer adds it and the policy stage verifies it. No
    issue is ever presented as human-written.
12. The publication does not use a human byline, a human persona, or first
    person singular.

## Corrections

13. Corrections are public. A correction is acknowledged in the next issue's
    "Corrections" section and appended to the original page. Silent edits to
    published facts are not permitted.
14. Feedback that identifies a factual error is treated as a correction
    candidate; the feedback stage records it and the editor prints it once
    the fact has been re-verified against the source.
